# Code of conduct

PGP Converter is a privacy tool, and people come to this repository with questions about keys, encryption and the safety of their messages, often for the first time. This code says how everyone here is expected to treat each other. It applies to the maintainer as much as to anyone else.

## Values

- Patience with questions. Nobody has to understand PGP before asking about it, and no question is too basic.
- Respect for privacy. Nobody should be asked to share a private key or passphrase they use, a decrypted message or personal details, and nobody should publish anyone else's.
- Disagreement about the work, not the person. Say what is wrong with an idea or a change, and why.
- Credit where it is due, and owning up to mistakes, the maintainer's included.

## Who is welcome

Everyone is welcome here, whatever their age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex, gender identity or expression, level of experience, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual orientation, family or social background, or the language they write best. The list names people who are often left out of projects like this one. It does not limit who this code protects.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, slurs and personal attacks, including ones made as jokes
- Belittling someone's question, skill or English, or remarks that assume someone does not belong here, such as assuming their gender
- Harassment: unwanted contact, following someone from one place to another, or carrying on after being asked to stop
- Threats, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others
- Sexual comments, images or advances
- Publishing someone's private information, such as their name, address or email, without their permission
- Asking anyone to post a private key, passphrase or decrypted message they use, or posting someone else's. A key pair made only to show a bug, and never used for anything else, may be shared.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion, through spam, trolling or repeated off-topic posts
- Retaliating against someone who reported a problem or helped with a report

## Where it applies

This code covers everything in this repository: issues, pull requests, reviews, commits, discussions and the comments on all of them. It also covers anyone speaking for the project elsewhere, and behaviour anywhere that targets people because of their part in the project.

## Reporting

The project uses GitHub's own tools. To report privately, go to GitHub Support, which reviews reports under [GitHub's Community Guidelines](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-community-guidelines) and does not pass them on to the maintainer:

- For an issue, pull request, discussion or comment, open its menu (the three dots, labelled Show options, or Discussion options on a discussion), choose Report content and fill in the form. If you are offered a choice, pick Report abuse to GitHub Support.
- For a person, including the maintainer, open their GitHub profile and choose Block or report, then Report abuse.
- If you cannot find either, use GitHub's [report abuse form](https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse).

To make sure the maintainer sees something, open an issue or mention @polyym in the conversation. Both are public, so anything that should stay private belongs with GitHub Support.

You do not have to be the person affected, and you do not have to be sure that something breaks this code. If it worries you, report it.

## What the maintainer does

When the maintainer sees or is told about something in this repository that breaks this code, they act on it. Depending on what happened, that can mean a reminder of this code, hiding, editing or deleting comments, locking a conversation, closing an issue or pull request, or blocking someone from the maintainer's GitHub account, which stops them opening issues or pull requests, or commenting, in any of the maintainer's repositories. The maintainer also reports threats, harassment and anything else serious to GitHub.

## About this code

This code was written for this project, following [Project Include's guide to writing codes of conduct](https://projectinclude.org/writing_cocs). Its lists of who is welcome and of what is not acceptable draw on the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/). Changes to it go through pull requests like any other change, and suggestions are welcome as an issue.
